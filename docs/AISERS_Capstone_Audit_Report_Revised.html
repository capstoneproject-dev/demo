Revised Capstone Readiness Review

AISERS System Audit Report

Requirements, implementation, security, permissions, database workflows, AI integration, paper consistency, and deployment readiness.

Project: NAAP–Villamor AISERS

Original audit date: July 28, 2026

Scope revision: August 6, 2026

Role interpretation: OSA Administrator and System Administrator are treated as one combined authorized account role.

Change policy: This revision changes audit scope only and does not modify application source files.

Scope clarification: Findings and implementation recommendations are limited to features required for the approved AISERS scope. Optional integrations and transaction methods outside that scope are not treated as missing requirements.

1. Role Interpretation Used in This Report

Approved account roles: Student, Organization Officer, and combined OSA/System Administrator.

This report treats OSA administration and system administration as responsibilities of the same authorized user. The existing osa_staff account type may represent the combined role, provided that it includes the administrative capabilities stated in the paper.

The rule that system-administration functions must not change OSA approval decisions is interpreted as a record-integrity rule: once the combined OSA/System Administrator finalizes an approval or rejection, the decision must not be silently overwritten. Corrections must use a new document version or a formally logged reconsideration process.

2. Executive Summary

Overall verdict: Not ready for production deployment. The combined OSA/System Administrator role is acceptable for the approved project interpretation, but critical authorization, data-integrity, security, and workflow requirements remain incomplete.

The system’s strongest areas are rental and service workflows, inventory, returns, fees and overtime, organization-scoped officer modules, dashboards, reports, and genuine AI-assisted descriptive analytics. The largest blockers identified by the original audit are unsafe OSA/System Administrator account creation, weak authorization in account-management endpoints, public exposure of sensitive files, absent MFA and CSRF protection, no protected audit log, mutable document approvals, missing document versioning, and an attendance duplication defect.

The active frontend is the HTML/CSS/JavaScript application backed by PHP and MySQL. The React/Vite directory is an unused prototype and is not the active frontend.

3. Critical Issues

1. Broken administrative authorization. Several Accounts APIs originally called only apiGuard(), which verified login but not the combined OSA/System Administrator role. This could allow a student or organization officer to perform account-management actions.
2. Public OSA/System Administrator self-provisioning. Administrator registration originally accepted any unused email and employee number without a verified staff invitation or approval by an existing authorized administrator.
3. Sensitive web-root exposure. Local HTTP testing found technical database material and uploaded documents under the public application tree. Sensitive submitted files require authenticated delivery and direct-access denial.
4. Approved decisions are mutable. An administrator can repeatedly change a submission between approved and rejected. Approved repository records are overwritten, and no immutable version history exists.
5. No comprehensive protected audit log. Dashboard activity assembled from mutable operational tables is not a complete append-only audit trail covering sensitive system actions.
6. Event registration can duplicate attendance. Registration may create a row without a check-in time, while check-in searches only for rows whose check-in date is current. Without a database uniqueness constraint, another row can be inserted for the same event and student.
7. Essential session and request protections are incomplete. CSRF protection, Secure cookies, session-ID regeneration, timeouts, and sensitive-action reauthentication are missing or incomplete. Privileged login MFA must also remain enforced.

5. Role and Permission Findings

  • Student: Accesses student events, rentals, printing, lockers, notifications, and personal records only.
  • Organization Officer: Remains a student account with active organization membership and organization-scoped authority.
  • OSA/System Administrator: May combine document review, compliance, account management, organization administration, configuration, audit-log viewing, and technical oversight.
  • The combined administrator account must not be publicly self-registered. Initial accounts should be seeded securely; additional accounts should require verified staff invitation or approval.
  • Organization ownership checks should scope every officer operation to the active organization.
  • Students do not submit organizational approval documents. Their file-upload workflow is for printing-service requests.

6. Approval Integrity for the Combined Administrator

  1. A pending submission may be reviewed and approved or rejected by an authorized OSA/System Administrator.
  2. The finalized decision and approved file become immutable.
  3. A correction or revised submission creates a new linked document version.
  4. If reconsideration is allowed, it must be a separate workflow with a mandatory reason and append-only audit entry.
  5. Account-management or configuration functions must never directly update finalized approval rows.

7. Database and Workflow Mismatches

  • A complete document-version history and general persistent notification model are absent.
  • The finalized document workflow does not prevent a later decision from overwriting an earlier decision.
  • Approving and later rejecting a document can leave inconsistent approved-repository data.
  • Event registration and actual participation are conflated in attendance_records.
  • Organization or event deletion can conflict with long-term participation and audit-record retention.

9. AI Assessment

Real AI-assisted analytics exists; ordinary totals and charts were not counted as AI.

  • Primary provider: Google Gemini.
  • Models: Gemini Flash-family models configured by the project.
  • Local fallback: Ollama.
  • Final fallback: Deterministic rule-based summaries.
  • Purpose: Descriptive narratives for revenue, participation, rentals/inventory, and document-workflow aggregates—not prediction or machine learning.
  • Data handling: The analytics builder should send only filtered totals, chart series, summaries, and limited event details, excluding direct student identifiers.
  • API-key handling: Provider keys must remain in server environment variables rather than source code.

10. Paper and Implementation Contradictions

  1. Project documentation should use one consistent deployment-readiness status.
  2. Terminology should consistently use “OSA/System Administrator” for the approved combined-role interpretation.
  3. The paper correctly assigns organizational document submission to officers and rental/service requests to students.
  4. Any claimed intermediate document-review stage must match the implemented workflow.
  5. Claimed announcement audiences must match the implemented targeting model.
  6. Offline attendance, financial audit records, policy updates, and formal compliance records should not be claimed without complete persistent workflows.
  7. The proposed ERD contains fields and tables not present in the deployed schema.
  8. Main dashboards are HTML/CSS/JavaScript clients backed by PHP JSON APIs; the architecture description should reflect that implementation.

11. Prioritized Fixes Before Defense

P0 — Immediate blockers

  1. Restrict every Accounts endpoint to the combined OSA/System Administrator role.
  2. Disable public administrator registration; use securely seeded accounts and verified staff invitations.
  3. Remove SQL dumps, technical documents, backups, and raw sensitive uploads from public access; disable directory indexes.
  4. Make finalized OSA decisions immutable and require a new version or logged reconsideration for corrections.
  5. Add CSRF protection, Secure cookies, session regeneration, timeouts, and sensitive-action reauthentication.
  6. Repair registration/check-in duplication and add database uniqueness protection.

P1 — Required integrity and administrator features

  1. Add comprehensive append-only protected audit logs accessible only to the authorized administrator.
  2. Add immutable document versions and archiving.
  3. Separate event registration state from verified participation and attendance.
  4. Add persistent role-targeted notifications with read and delivery states where required by the final scope.
  5. Enforce MFA for every OSA/System Administrator account.
  6. Complete administrator capabilities without allowing direct alteration of finalized approvals.

P2 — Deployment hardening

  1. Validate upload MIME type, signature, size, and authorized download access.
  2. Use environment secrets and a least-privilege production database account.
  3. Configure and verify TLS 1.3, HSTS, encryption at rest, and secure backups.
  4. Add centralized error handling and security headers.
  5. Replace runtime schema changes with controlled database migrations.

P3 — Defense alignment and quality

  1. Use “OSA/System Administrator” consistently in the interface, manual, diagrams, and defense explanation.
  2. Implement only the remaining workflows and notification audiences confirmed by the final approved paper.
  3. Describe AI accurately as assisted descriptive analytics.
  4. Remove the unused React/Vite prototype and mock-data controls from the production package.
  5. Create a role-specific user manual and documented responsive-browser test results.
  6. Repair broken navigation and hard-coded local deployment paths.

12. Defense Position

The defensible role explanation is:

“OSA personnel also perform authorized system-administration functions. OSA Administrator and System Administrator are functional responsibilities under one secured account role, not separate user types. Finalized OSA decisions remain immutable, and every sensitive administrative action is audited.”

This combined-role design is acceptable for the approved project interpretation if it is used consistently and the missing security, administrative, audit, and record-integrity controls are implemented.
